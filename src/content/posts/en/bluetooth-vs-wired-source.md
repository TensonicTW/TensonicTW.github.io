## The Problem

When installing a karaoke or background-music system on site, a common question comes up: "Can I just play from my phone over Bluetooth — will the sound quality suffer?" The question actually spans two separate issues: the codec limitations inherent to Bluetooth transmission itself, and whether that quality difference is actually audible in the given use case.

## Analysis: The Limits of Bluetooth Audio Transmission

Bluetooth audio transmission compresses audio data through a codec before sending it. Common codecs include SBC (Bluetooth's built-in standard), AAC, and aptX, each with different compression ratios and degrees of quality loss. Even with a higher-quality codec, Bluetooth transmission is still lossy compression — compared to a wired connection (analogue RCA, optical, coaxial), there is, in principle, always some quality loss.

Whether that loss is actually noticeable in practice depends on a few variables:

- **Dynamic range of the content.** Vocal-dominant content with a narrower dynamic range (like karaoke backing tracks) makes Bluetooth compression loss less noticeable; music with wide dynamic range and rich detail (classical, Hi-Fi recordings) makes the loss easier to perceive.
- **Background noise in the listening environment.** Private rooms and banquet venues typically have some level of ambient noise and conversation, which masks part of the detail lost to Bluetooth compression — this is part of why karaoke settings generally tolerate Bluetooth sound quality well.
- **Bluetooth version of the device.** A newer Bluetooth version (such as V5.4) outperforms older versions in transmission stability and latency, reducing dropouts or lag issues — but that's a separate matter from audio codec compression ratio; a newer Bluetooth version doesn't mean quality loss disappears.

## Choosing a Source Interface

Take Tensonic's S-6000 as an example: it supports both Bluetooth V5.4 and wired inputs (analogue RCA, optical, coaxial, USB) simultaneously. The point of this multi-interface design is to let users choose the appropriate source method for the situation, rather than relying on Bluetooth alone:

- **Ordinary karaoke backing-track playback:** connecting a phone over Bluetooth is entirely adequate — convenience takes priority, and the quality loss falls within what most listeners notice.
- **High-quality background music or playback of live recordings:** a wired input is preferable, especially when paired with a source device with a high-resolution DAC like the TS-DMP8 — only a wired connection lets the source's sound-quality advantage come through fully; Bluetooth compression would otherwise cancel out the benefit of that higher-spec source.
- **Venues with explicit sound-quality expectations** (spaces marketed on a Hi-Fi experience): avoid routing a high-quality source through Bluetooth, which effectively cancels out the investment made in that source equipment.

## Method

A recommended split by use case:

1. Everyday karaoke backing tracks, casual phone-music playback: Bluetooth is fine — prioritize convenience.
2. Fixed background-music playback or venues with specific sound-quality requirements: prioritize a wired source and confirm the source's own specifications (DAC model, THD, dynamic range).
3. If a single venue has both needs, choose an amplifier that supports Bluetooth and multiple wired inputs simultaneously, and switch sources based on the current use case rather than being locked into one option.

## Follow-Up Recommendations

When planning a system, first clarify the venue's primary use case — karaoke entertainment versus background-music/Hi-Fi playback — before deciding the priority order for source-interface configuration, rather than applying a single source-interface assumption across every use case.
